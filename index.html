AI for Cybersecurity and Privacy

Foundations of modern machine learning, adversarial security, privacy-preserving learning, fairness, and trustworthy AI.

Instructor: Dr. Mohamed Seif

Course Objective

This course introduces the principles and applications of artificial intelligence (AI) for cybersecurity and privacy. Beginning with a hands-on introduction to deep neural networks and AI modeling, students develop the foundations needed to understand modern AI systems and their applications in cybersecurity.

Building on these foundations, the course examines the security of AI systems, including adversarial machine learning, data poisoning, model evasion, model extraction, privacy inference attacks, and defenses. It also explores privacy-preserving machine learning, explainability, fairness, and ethical considerations, emphasizing the opportunities, limitations, and tradeoffs involved in deploying trustworthy AI in security-critical settings.

Course Structure

Module 1 — A Crash Course on Modern Machine Learning (ML)

Core ML and deep learning concepts, neural networks, optimization, training, and evaluation.

Module 2 — Robust and Adversarial ML

Adversarial machine learning and techniques for attacks and defenses.

Module 3 — Privacy Preserving Learning

Basics of differential privacy and privacy-preserving machine learning.

Module 4 — Fairness in ML

Notions of fairness and algorithmic techniques for learning fair classifiers.

Module 6 — Emerging Topics in AI

Transformers: Self-attention, positional encoding, and architectures.

Module 6 — Student Project Presentations

Presentation and discussion of semester-long research projects.
